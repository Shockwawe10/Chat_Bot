# Glossary

Terms used in the demo knowledge base, the golden dataset and the tests. Each term is a `###` heading so `tests/data` can count them.

## Solar basics

### PV module
A photovoltaic panel that turns sunlight into electricity. Mounting systems carry PV modules; the company in the demo domain does not make modules.

### kWp (kilowatt-peak)
The rated power of PV modules under standard test conditions. Plant size is given in kWp or MWp (1 MWp = 1000 kWp). The knowledge base always writes these units as `kWp` and `MWp`, with a space after the number.

### Tilt angle
The angle between a PV module and the horizontal plane, in degrees. A larger tilt sheds snow better but catches more wind.

## Mounting systems

### Mounting system
The steel or aluminium structure that holds PV modules in place and transfers their weight, wind load and snow load to the ground or the roof.

### Ground-mount system
A mounting system on open land, fixed to the ground with posts or piles. Usually used for plants from hundreds of kWp to many MWp.

### Flat-roof ballasted system
A mounting system for flat roofs that is held down by weight (ballast, usually concrete blocks) instead of fixings that pierce the roof membrane.

### East-west system
A flat-roof layout where modules face east and west in pairs at a low tilt. It fits more modules on a roof and catches less wind than a south-facing layout.

### Pitched-roof system
A mounting system for sloped roofs: rails fixed to the roof structure with hooks or brackets, with modules parallel to the roof surface.

### Carport system
A mounting system that is also a parking shelter: modules form the roof over parking spaces.

### Rammed post
A steel post driven into the ground by a ramming machine, without concrete. The fastest foundation for a ground-mount system when the soil allows it.

### Screw pile
A steel pile with a helical blade that is screwed into the ground. Used where ramming is not possible, for example in stony soil.

### Ballast
Weight, usually concrete blocks, that keeps a flat-roof system in place against wind uplift.

## Corrosion protection

### Corrosivity category
A class from ISO 9223 that describes how aggressive the environment is to metal: C1 (very low), C2 (low), C3 (medium), C4 (high), C5 (very high) and CX (extreme). A mounting system is rated for the highest category it is designed to withstand. Coastal and industrial sites are often C4 or C5.

### Hot-dip galvanising
A coating made by dipping finished steel parts into molten zinc. The zinc layer protects the steel and is measured in µm.

### Zinc-magnesium coating
A zinc coating with added magnesium and aluminium, applied to steel strip before forming. At the same thickness it resists corrosion better than plain zinc, especially in salty air.

### Coating thickness
The average thickness of a protective coating, in µm. The knowledge base writes micrometres as `µm`.

## Loads

### Wind load
The force that wind puts on modules and the structure, including uplift. A mounting system is designed for a maximum wind speed, given in m/s.

### Snow load
The weight of snow on the modules, given in kN/m². A mounting system is designed for a maximum snow load.

### Structural calculation
An engineering calculation that proves a mounting system will carry the wind load, snow load and its own weight at a specific site.

## Project stages

### Site survey
A visit to the customer's site to measure it, check access, the roof or the ground, and collect data for design.

### Geotechnical survey
Soil investigation for a ground-mount project, including pull-out tests. It decides the foundation type and post length.

### Pull-out test
A test where a sample post is pulled upward with a measured force to check how well the soil holds it.

### Design
The engineering stage: layout, structural calculation, drawings and the bill of materials.

### Supply
Manufacturing and delivering the mounting system to the site.

### Installation
Building the mounting system on site and fixing the modules to it.

### Commercial offer
A written offer with the price and scope for one specific project. The company has no public price list; every price comes from a commercial offer.
